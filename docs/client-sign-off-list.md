# Client sign-off list

Extracted from the supplied page briefs. All entries remain unconfirmed. Review these with the corresponding page before production.

## about.md
1. Whether the lifting equipment is owned or hired, which affects section 6 and the whole equipment page
2. The characterisation of fitters running jobs on site and dealing with the client's own customers, which is drawn from the client's own written answer but is stronger here in their own voice
3. Whether they are comfortable publishing the headcount breakdown. Some companies prefer not to reveal their size. If that is the case, this page loses its best material and will need rethinking rather than trimming
1. **Year founded.** Sixty years combined is a team statistic, not a company age. A founding year would sit naturally in section 2 and is the most common thing a cautious customer looks for
2. **Who runs it.** Gary is on the business card with no role attached. A named director with a job title converts better than an anonymous company
3. **What DS stands for.** Possibly initials, possibly nothing. If there is a story it belongs in section 2, and if there is not, leave it alone
4. **Team photography.** Section 2 specifies a group shot and section 4 an installation shot. Both are easy to take and both are currently placeholders
5. **Accreditations and memberships.** FORS, BAR, ISO, trade bodies. If any exist they belong on this page as a logo row beneath section 3
6. **Insurance and goods in transit cover.** Currently unstated anywhere on the site. Once supplied, the natural home is a fourth stat in section 3
7. **Company registration and VAT numbers.** For the footer rather than this page, but they come from the same conversation
8. **Operator licence**, and whether international work runs on their own licence or through partners

## contact.md
1. Whether all four channels are genuinely monitored 24/7, or whether that applies to the phone only. The page currently implies the phone and says nothing about the others, which is correct, but the client should confirm it
2. Whether they want deliveries and collections at the Charing address at all, and whether ringing ahead is the actual instruction
3. Company registration and VAT numbers, which belong in the footer and are usually found on this page by anyone checking the company is real

## quote.md
1. **Response time.** The form currently promises nothing. It should promise something, because "we will get back to you" converts worse than "within one working day". Ask what they can actually commit to and then hold them to it
2. Which inbox quote submissions land in. `accounts@` is wrong for this and the enquiry address question is still open
3. Whether photograph uploads are wanted, or whether they would rather people emailed them
4. Whether anyone monitors enquiries out of hours, given the 24/7 claim sitting directly beneath the form. If the answer is that the phone is 24/7 but the inbox is not, the aside wording should say so plainly rather than implying otherwise

## recent-jobs.md
1. All four job narratives, which currently contain only destination and job type
2. Whether the client is contractually able to name locations at all. Interior designers and their private clients are frequently covered by confidentiality, and Lake Como plus a named designer could be a problem where Lake Como alone is not
3. Whether any client will give a testimonial, which would change this page more than photography would
4. The France entry, which is the vaguest of the four and may cover several jobs rather than one

## service-business-deliveries.md
1. Dedicated rather than consolidated as a standard, since any groupage work would contradict it
2. Pricing regular work on the route rather than per job
3. Setting up a new route within roughly two weeks
4. Whether accounts get a named contact formally, or whether that is simply what happens at their size
5. Multi drop routes, which are not mentioned anywhere in the discovery answers

## service-equipment-transport.md
1. Access surveys on larger equipment jobs, and who carries them out
2. Whether the Moffett and HIAB are owned or hired in. The whole of section 5 depends on this and the claim must be pulled if they are hired
3. Collecting direct from manufacturers abroad, as opposed to receiving from a freight forwarder
4. Whether they will work alongside a client's engineers on site during a commissioning visit
5. Any weight ceiling they want stated, since the page currently implies capability up to 26 tonne vehicle capacity without naming an item limit

## service-exhibition-transport.md
1. Full event kit for multi venue tours and roadshows
2. Empty case storage and return during a show, which is a specific venue service
3. Whether supervisors attend site as standard or only on larger jobs
4. Storing stands between shows, including whether this is charged as ordinary warehousing
5. European customs and carnet handling for event goods, which matters more here than on any other service page and is currently unanswered

## service-office-relocations.md
1. Partitions and screens as a handled item type
2. Server and IT equipment, which carries different risk and may need separate confirmation
3. Packaging and waste removal on completion
4. Site surveys as standard practice, and who carries them out
5. Labelling against a floor plan as a standard process
6. Phased moves, and whether they have run them

## service-private-deliveries.md
1. Whether private and individual work is accepted, and whether they want more of it
2. Pianos and large instruments, which need specialist handling and may be a firm no
3. Two person handling as standard on private jobs
4. Packaging removal from a domestic property
5. Assembly in a domestic setting, as opposed to commercial installation
6. Collection from auction houses, which often have their own access and release procedures
7. Whether same day applies to private work or only to business accounts

## service-retail-display-transport.md
1. Multi site rollouts as a service they actively run, and the realistic maximum number of locations
2. Consolidating a campaign from manufacturers and printers before distribution
3. Working to shopping centre and landlord access windows, including whether they have done this
4. Installation of retail fixtures specifically, as opposed to furniture and office installation
5. Campaign recovery and return to store as a bookable service
6. Call off fulfilment, where stock is held and released on request
7. Full store fit packages
8. Packaging removal on completion

## services-hub.md
1. The private items answer, which is blocked on the larger decision
2. Partner coverage for destinations beyond their own fleet, and whether they are comfortable saying so openly
3. Consolidating jobs arriving from multiple suppliers, which appears on several pages and has not yet been confirmed once

## storage.md
1. Warehouse location and whether it is at the Charing address or elsewhere
2. Capacity, in whatever unit they think in. Square footage, pallet spaces or racking bays
3. Security arrangements: alarmed, monitored, gated, CCTV
4. Insurance position for goods held in store, and the cover level
5. Whether the facility is bonded, which matters for anything moving in and out of Europe
6. Receiving deliveries from third party suppliers on the client's behalf
7. Checking goods in against a job reference or schedule
8. Call off in stages
9. Whether storage is charged separately or absorbed into project pricing
10. Minimum and maximum storage periods, if any
11. Whether they will store goods for customers who are not also using them for transport
